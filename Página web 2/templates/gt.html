How to use this page

BE AN EARTH SYSTEM TREND DETECTIVE

1. Introduction

EarthTrend AI explores environmental and climate data to identify long-term trends and changes across different regions of the world.

2. Databases

The project uses NASA and NOAA Earth observation datasets:

  • GPM IMERG: Precipitation.
  • MERRA-2 / NASA POWER: Atmospheric variables.
  • MODIS: Vegetation, land surface temperature, evapotranspiration, and productivity.
  • GLDAS: Soil moisture, temperature, and water cycle variables.
  • NOAA OISST: Sea surface temperature.

3. Analysis

  • Time Series: Visualize environmental changes over time.
  • Linear Regression: Estimate trends, slopes, and statistical significance.
  • Statistical Tests: Mann-Kendall, Seasonal Mann-Kendall, and stationarity analysis.
  • Heat Maps: Explore spatial distributions of environmental variables.
  • Interactive Globe: Select locations and explore available data.

Neural Network Overview

How does EarthTrend AI work?

EarthTrend AI uses a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) to analyze nine environmental variables simultaneously. By processing historical observations of soil, water, and atmospheric conditions, the network learns temporal patterns and relationships within the environmental system. These patterns are used to forecast the future evolution of each variable across two prediction horizons: one month and twelve months.

Unlike approaches that analyze each variable independently, EarthTrend AI integrates multiple environmental signals to generate a multivariable forecast.

Two prediction horizons

One-Month Prediction

The network analyzes the previous 36 months of environmental observations to predict the following month. This short-term forecast evaluates the model's ability to anticipate near-future changes in the environmental system.

36 months of historical data → 1 month forecast

Twelve-Month Prediction

The network analyzes the previous 24 months of environmental observations to predict the evolution of all nine variables over the following twelve months. This horizon evaluates its ability to capture longer-term temporal patterns and seasonal variations.

24 months of historical data → 12 month forecast
